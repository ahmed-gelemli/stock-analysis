## 2. Business and financials

platform that keeps donor hearts, lungs, and livers metabolically active in transit -
plus an increasingly important logistics/aviation service layer (the National OCS
Program, NOP) that handles organ transport door-to-door. Company states ~20% share of a
domestic TAM of ~17,900 yearly heart/lung/liver transplants, growing ~7%/yr
[company-stated, via secondary source, not independently verified this session].

## 6. Valuation

**Method 1: Reverse DCF (terminal-year approach).**
Assumptions: WACC 15% (CAPM: 4.65% 10yr UST + 1.88 beta x 5.5% assumed ERP), terminal
FCF margin 25%, perpetuity growth 3%.
```
Implied 5-yr revenue CAGR to justify current EV ($2.415B): ~28%
```
Output: current price requires a growth path faster than either consensus or a
mechanical deceleration of current guidance delivers.

**Method 2: Explicit 5-year DCF.**
Assumptions: same 15% WACC. Revenue $747M (2026E, guide midpoint) -> $874M (2027E,
consensus) -> $996M -> $1,115M -> $1,227M (assumed deceleration to 14%/12%/10%). FCF
margin ramping 15% (2026E, depressed by disclosed reinvestment) -> 24% (2030E, terminal),
tracking management's framing that the compression is temporary. ~39M diluted shares
assumed (between the current 34.6M basic and Q2's actual 40.7M diluted count - itself a
modeling assumption).
```
PV of explicit FCF ~= $633M
Terminal value (Gordon growth, g=3.5%), PV ~= $1,316M
EV ~= $1,949M -> equity ~= $2,440M -> fair value ~= $63/share
```
Output: **~$63**, about 25% below spot.

**Reconciliation.** Both methods point the same direction - the current price is not
obviously cheap on a bottom-up basis at a WACC that reflects TMDX's actual measured
beta. Weighted moderately, not heavily: a 15% WACC assumes the market prices litigation-
driven volatility as a permanent feature of the discount rate, which may overstate the
true cost of capital if the overhang resolves without material findings (over a year
with no confirmed regulatory action so far). **Anti-anchoring check:** the $63 DCF output
is not within 5% of spot ($83.89) or the sell-side mean ($97.30), so this doesn't read as
anchored - but the conclusion is genuinely WACC-sensitive: a lower, still-defensible 11-
12% WACC would push fair value back up near or above spot.

**Implied-multiple cross-check:** at $63, implied EV/Sales (TTM) ~= 2.6x, below the
current 3.61x and far below the ~8x the stock traded at near its 52-week high. At spot,
3.61x TTM sales for a still-20%+ grower with 60% gross margins is not rich in isolation -
the multiple has already re-rated hard from momentum-growth to controversy-discount
territory.

**Return decomposition:** not meaningful to decompose in the standard growth/multiple/
buyback format here - the entire gap between the DCF output and spot is a discount-rate/
growth-path disagreement, not a re-rating bet, which is the more important flag than the
number itself.

## 7. Bull case / bear case

**Bull** (implicit in the 6-strong-buy/2-buy consensus lean, and in Needham's continued
Buy rating even after cutting its target): OCS Kidney, if it clears trials and launches,
roughly doubles TAM into kidney transplants, the largest organ category by volume.
Service-segment margin is already inflecting (27% -> 35% in one quarter) as the
aviation/logistics buildout scales - the durable, harder-to-replicate moat (TMDX
effectively owns organ transport logistics, not just the device). International
expansion (Italy plant) and ENHANCE/DENOVO trials are unguided upside. The litigation
overhang is a year-plus old with no confirmed regulatory action - bulls argue it's fading
noise, not a live threat.

**Bear** (Scorpion Capital's thesis, still live via the pending *Jewik* class action):
revenue growth was built partly on coercive/overbilling commercial practices now under
undisclosed regulatory scrutiny, and that remains untested and unresolved; a formal DOJ
action would reprice the stock sharply regardless of the underlying transplant-volume
growth story. Separately, NRP is a structurally cheaper technology TMDX can't counter
through its hospital-only sales model, capping the long-run share ceiling even absent any
legal issue. Margin guidance has already been cut once this year (Q2) on top of a Q1
miss - two disappointments in two quarters is a pattern, not noise, on the "temporary
reinvestment" framing management is offering.

**Where I land:** no edge on the growth-rate debate either way - both DCF methods land
below spot at a defensible WACC, but that WACC itself is the real judgment call. The
actual point of disagreement with a purely bullish read is that I don't think the
litigation/regulatory overhang is priced as cleanly as "already in the stock" - a name
carrying ~23% short interest and 9.5x days-to-cover a year into an unresolved fraud
allegation is still being actively fought over, not settled.

## 10. What would change my mind

   days later), declines meaningfully from the current ~23%-of-float estimate while price
   holds above $85** - would indicate the market is de-risking the litigation thesis,
   supporting a shift toward bull.
