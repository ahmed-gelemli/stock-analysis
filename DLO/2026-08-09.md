## The call in three lines

1. **What the market is pricing:** at $14.40, the current price implies roughly a **16%
   5-year gross-profit CAGR** (reverse DCF, 12% WACC) — below both 2026 guidance
   (22.5-27.5% GP growth) and FY2025 actual delivery (37%). The market is pricing
   meaningful deceleration and/or a derating, not a continuation of the current trajectory.
2. **Where I differ and why:** guidance, taken at face value, supports a base-case value
   around $17-18, and independently-built DCF scenarios land there too. But a formal
   risk/reward test (upside-to-base ÷ downside-to-bear) comes out **below 2:1** — the bear
   case here is genuinely severe (take-rate compression, Argentina/Brazil regulatory
   overhang, recurring short-seller allegations), so "cheap on guidance" isn't the same as
   "favorable risk/reward at this exact price."
3. **What would prove me wrong:** Q2 2026 results, reported Wednesday Aug 13, 2026 after
   market close, are the near-term test. A repeat of Q1's pattern (revenue in-line,
   Adjusted EBITDA/margin miss) would confirm the bear case; take-rate stabilization would
   support the bull case.

## 7. Bull case / bear case

**Bull** (implicit in the ~9-17 analysts at "Buy"/"Strong Buy" consensus, avg PT
~$17.35-$18.00): TPV growth of 50%+ for six straight quarters is the real signal; take-rate
decline is a natural consequence of landing larger merchants and expanding into new
payment methods/countries, not commoditization. Operating leverage shows up in 2H26 as
guided. Net-cash balance sheet, 97% FCF conversion in FY2025, fresh $300M buyback give
downside support. At 7.5x NTM Gross Profit for 50%+ TPV growth, the multiple is cheap.

**Bear** (Hollenden Square Research, and independently the Q1 2026 critical reviews read
in full): opex grew faster than revenue in Q1 2026 (58% vs 55%) — not what a "high-quality
scalable tech business" should do. Take rate has roughly halved from its 2022-2024 level
with no sign of stabilizing. Revenue concentration in a small number of large merchants
gives those merchants outsized pricing leverage — arguably why take rate keeps falling.
Argentina and Brazil are named by management itself as ongoing risks, not resolved ones.
Real, unresolved pattern: two separate short-seller reports (2023, 2025) with overlapping
allegations about FX-exposure reporting and related-party transactions, plus an open
Argentine regulatory investigation whose current status could not be confirmed.

Not fully rebutted — the opex-growing-faster-than-revenue point is a current-quarter
fact, not a stale allegation.

## 9. Risks and frictions

**Asymmetry test:** (upside to Base $17.38) ÷ (downside to Bear $9.21) = 20.7% ÷ 36.1% =
**0.57 — below the 2:1 threshold.** At $14.40, the base-case reward does not compensate
2:1 for the bear-case risk as strictly defined. The ratio reaches 2:1 only near **$11.93**
or below (or under materially softer bear-case assumptions). This cuts against the "cheap
on guidance multiples" read in Section 6 — both are true at once.

**Liquidity:** ADV roughly 1.2M-3.6M shares/day ($17M-$50M), optionable, 344 listed
contracts — adequate for a $4.2B name, not a microcap liquidity risk.

**Event risk:** Q2 2026 earnings sit inside every horizon discussed here. IV30 of 65%
already prices this in; don't treat the pre-earnings price as a stable base.

**Legal/regulatory:** unresolved Argentine FX/capital-control investigation and 2023
securities class action status not confirmed current — an open tail risk.

**Currency/tax:** dLocal reports in USD but transacts across dozens of EM currencies
undergoing active devaluation (Argentina most acute); take-rate decline is a direct,
already-observed transmission mechanism. Brazil's tax environment separately flagged by
management.

**Short side:** short interest only ~5.3% of shares outstanding, 4.65 days-to-cover — no
squeeze dynamics either way; also not a crowded short.

Frictions: US wash-sale rules, short- vs long-term capital gains treatment, and
foreign-issuer dividend withholding considerations apply (DLO is a direct Cayman-domiciled
Nasdaq listing, not a traditional ADR, but withholding treatment can still differ by
jurisdiction). Rules vary by jurisdiction — confirm locally before acting.

## 10. What would change my mind

1. **Q2 2026 results, Aug 13, 2026 (after close):** if Adjusted EBITDA misses consensus
   again (as in Q1, $60.5M vs ~$72M) *and* TPV growth decelerates below the 50% guidance
   floor, that confirms the bear case. An EBITDA beat with take-rate stabilization
   supports the bull case.
2. **Take rate:** stabilization or a tick up for two consecutive quarters (breaking the
   1.05%→0.88%→0.84% slide) supports management's "scale dynamics" framing.
3. **Legal/regulatory:** a formal charge, fine, or settlement disclosed in a future
   6-K/20-F tied to the Argentine probe or Hollenden Square allegations validates the
   governance-risk bear case. The next 20-F (expected ~March 2027) showing no material
   development would meaningfully de-risk the overhang.
4. **Price level:** a close below **$11.93** for two consecutive weeks on above-average
   volume indicates the market has moved to pricing something closer to the bear case as
   its new base case.
