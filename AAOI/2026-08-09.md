## The call in three lines

1. **What the market is pricing:** at $135.63, on a terminal-year model with a 13% discount rate,
   a 24x exit multiple on FCF and a 16% terminal FCF margin, the price requires roughly **47%
   compound annual revenue growth for five years to about $6.8bn**. On friendlier inputs (11%
   discount, 25x exit, 18% FCF margin) it still requires **38%/yr to $5.1bn**. Either way the
   price embeds both a revenue outcome and a margin outcome that must land together.
2. **Where I differ and why:** the demand is real and I do not dispute it (data center revenue
   +140% YoY, 800G volumes more than doubled sequentially, capacity is the binding constraint).
   But **the unit economics are not improving with scale**: incremental operating margin was
   **-9.8%** YoY, GAAP gross margin **fell** 30.3% to 27.7%, adjusted EBITDA was **-$0.5m**, and
   the reported "return to non-GAAP profitability" is produced entirely by a **$14.3m notional tax
   add-back**. Strip it and Q2 was a non-GAAP pre-tax loss of $8.8m. The terminal FCF margin the
   price requires (16-18%) is roughly double anything merchant optics has historically earned.
3. **What would prove me wrong:** GAAP gross margin above 32% with positive GAAP operating income
   at the Q3 print, plus the Digicomm receivable falling in absolute dollars. That combination
   would make the margin compression a ramp artifact rather than structural, and the base case
   moves toward $200+.

## 7. Bull case / bear case

**Bull** (held by Needham at $190 and Raymond James at $151, both ATM sales agents, plus momentum
and AI-infrastructure funds; consensus rating "Buy", n=6):

The optical transceiver market is the physical bottleneck of AI scale-out, and AAOI is one of a
handful of vertically integrated suppliers with in-house laser fabrication, which is the scarce
asset. Demand exceeds capacity through mid-2027 and the company is buying that capacity now, which
is exactly what management should do with an equity currency at 19x sales. The $1.03bn raised at
$103 and $161 is not dilution so much as a nearly free option on a market inflection, funded by
the market's own enthusiasm. Data center revenue at +140% with 800G doubling sequentially is a
shipping number, not a narrative. Margin compression is a ramp artifact: yields improve, absorption
improves, and 1.6T mix carries better pricing. Amazon signing a warrant tied to $4bn of purchases
is the strongest available third-party validation and it is contractual. If FY2027 revenue reaches
$2.5bn at 33% gross margin with opex growing at a third of revenue growth, EPS approaches $4 and
the stock trades at 34x on numbers that are still accelerating.

**This is taken seriously. The revenue is real, the constraint is real, and the margin call is the
disputable input.**

**Bear** (not the Culper thesis, which is stale; this is built from the current filings):

Strip the accounting and Q2 was gross margin down 260bps YoY, negative incremental operating
margin, negative adjusted EBITDA, and a non-GAAP "profit" that exists only because of a $14.3m tax
benefit the company did not receive in cash. Simultaneously receivables sit at 149 days, inventory
at 183 days, and 67% of receivables are with one distributor on deliberately extended terms while
that distributor is 43% of revenue. The company consumed $707m of cash in six months against $343m
of revenue and funded it by selling $1.03bn of stock, $646m of which went out in Q2 at an average
of $161, above where the stock trades today. And the terminal valuation requires a 16% to 18% free
cash flow margin from a business model whose historical gross margin ceiling is 30%. **The demand
can be entirely real and the stock can still be worth half of this.**

Not rebutted: the negative incremental operating margin is a current-quarter fact, not an
allegation.

## 9. Risks and frictions

**Asymmetry test:**

```
Asymmetry = (Base $115 - spot $135.63) / (spot $135.63 - Bear $45)
          = -20.63 / 90.63 = -0.23        [computed]
```

**Negative, and far below the 2:1 threshold.** On these numbers the risk/reward does not support
action at this price. A 2:1 ratio against this base and bear is reached only at or below **$68.30**.
Stated plainly: the base case is the disputable input. A bull who models 33% gross margins gets a
different base and therefore a different answer, and that single assumption is where the
disagreement lives.

**Liquidity:** not a constraint. ADV 8.7m shares, roughly $1.2bn/day at current prices, 24.0m
shares on 2026-08-07. Optionable with a deep chain (2,108 contracts). Execution is not the risk
here; volatility is.

**Concentration:** three customers are 92% of revenue; one distributor is 43% of revenue and 67%
of receivables. A single customer decision moves the entire P&L.

**Dilution:** the most probable value transfer in this name. 70.8% share growth in 19 months,
roughly $60m of ATM capacity remaining, 7.95m warrant shares at $23.70, 2.88m convertible shares
at $43.31, and guided share count already 92.8m against 84.4m outstanding.

**Volatility:** 140% realized. A 12% single-day move is a 1-sigma event and happens often here.
Position sizing that ignores this is a larger practical risk than the thesis being wrong.

**Event risk:** the next earnings date is unverified. Anything sized to that event should wait for
confirmation from IR or an 8-K.

**Short side, if the read is bearish:** shorting is not the mirror image of buying. Short interest
is 11.8% of shares outstanding on a float that was not retrieved, so the true percentage is higher.
**Borrow fee and hard-to-borrow status were not retrieved and must be checked before assuming a
short is economic.** Days-to-cover of 1.14 argues against a violent squeeze, but a stock that ran
77% in seven sessions can do it again on flow alone, and losses are unbounded. A correct thesis
with wrong timing is still a loss.

**Frictions and taxes:** a 1-to-12-month holding period in the US produces short-term capital gains
at ordinary income rates; wash-sale rules apply to loss harvesting; options on a 120-IV name carry
very large premiums, so any options expression is paying for that volatility rather than getting it
free. Jurisdiction, tax status, and any trading restrictions (employer blackout, insider status,
regulated-person rules) are unknown to this analysis. **Rules vary by jurisdiction, confirm locally.**

**Good company or good stock?** Different questions, different answers. *Good company:* a real
business at a real bottleneck, growing 86% YoY, capacity constrained, validated by a contractual
Amazon relationship, and also a company with no operating profit in four years, falling gross
margin as volume rises, and reported profitability that depends on a tax assumption. Legitimate
business, unproven unit economics at scale. *Good stock at $135.63:* on these numbers, no, and the
asymmetry is negative. Tempered by two things: the bull case is genuinely 2x from here, and the 3-
to-12-month path is driven more by the sector's multiple than by AAOI's income statement. If the
AI-optics complex holds these multiples, this valuation will be wrong for a while.

## 10. What would change my mind

1. **Gross margin and GAAP operating income at the Q3 print** (indicated ~2026-11-05, unverified).
   If **GAAP gross margin prints above 32% with positive GAAP operating income**, the ramp-artifact
   explanation is correct, operating leverage is real, the margin skepticism is wrong, and the base
   case moves toward $200+. Conversely, if **GAAP gross margin prints below 28%** on revenue inside
   the $255m-$290m guide, the negative incremental margin is structural rather than transitional
   and the bear case is live.
2. **Digicomm receivable in the Q3 10-Q.** Falling **below $180m in absolute dollars** while CATV
   revenue grows means the channel is selling through and the extended-terms concern resolves.
   Rising **above $250m** means revenue is being financed into a distributor's warehouse, and
   reported CATV revenue should be treated as low-quality.
3. **ATM refresh.** A new or upsized equity distribution agreement **above $500m** within the next
   two quarters while the stock is below $150 says management needs capital at prices below its own
   Q2 average of $161, and the dilution path is worse than modeled.
4. **The non-GAAP tax add-back.** If Q3 non-GAAP net income lands inside the guided $10.1m-$24.0m
   but again **depends on a tax benefit exceeding 50% of the total**, the profitability milestone is
   an accounting artifact for a second consecutive quarter and all forward non-GAAP guidance should
   be discounted accordingly.
5. **Price level.** Two consecutive weekly closes **below $76.52** (the 2026-07-29 low) on
   above-average volume would mean the post-earnings rally failed entirely and the July drawdown is
   resuming.
