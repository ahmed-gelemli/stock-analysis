## 2. Business and financials

personal-loan platforms, point-of-sale) route declined credit applications through
Pagaya's models; Pagaya approves a subset, funds the loans (largely off its own balance
sheet via securitization), and earns fees plus a funding spread. Auto is now the primary
growth engine (annualized run-rate network volume $4.8B, Q2'26 release).

## 7. Bull case / bear case

**Bull** (held by B. Riley/Hal Goetsch, PT raised $32->$36 post-print; implicit in the
"Strong Buy" 10-analyst consensus aggregated by stockanalysis.com): two consecutive
beat-and-raise quarters, durable GAAP profitability, >30% YoY Auto-vertical growth, and a
Fitch outlook moving the right direction. At 3.9x forward EBITDA against a peer set trading
2-6x richer for comparable or slower growth, this is a statistically cheap, de-risking
AI-lending platform the market hasn't caught up to.

**Bear** (Iceberg Research, Feb 2025, though it covered its short by May 2025; implicitly
the plaintiffs' firms still soliciting investors for the related investigation): Pagaya's
core business is monetizing loans its own partners already rejected - adverse selection
into the weakest part of the credit stack, by construction. It funds that book through
securitization, and its own Q2'26 release admits the price it's paying for that funding is
rising, compressing the one margin metric (FRLPC%) that determines whether growth is
value-accretive. A credible short seller alleged the company used a captive fund to
quietly absorb the riskiest tranches of its own deals while telling the market those
tranches went to independent investors - never, to my knowledge, formally resolved, just
outlasted. Insiders control ~81% of the vote through 10-vote Class B shares, leaving public
shareholders essentially no governance leverage if that pattern repeats. The stock has
proven, twice in 18 months, that it can lose a quarter to a third of its value in days on
exactly this kind of news. The cheap multiple isn't a market mistake - it's the market
correctly pricing tail risk in a business model that concentrates credit risk by design.

**Where I land:** the bull case's earnings evidence (two real, Tier-1-sourced beat-and-
raise quarters) is stronger than the bear case's unresolved-but-unproven governance
allegation, which is why the base case leans constructive. But the bear case isn't fully
retired - the FRLPC compression is a *current*, *disclosed*, non-speculative data point,
and it's exactly the mechanism the bear case predicts. Specific disagreement with "market
efficiently prices this at 3.9x forever": two consecutive guide-beats plus a
ratings-outlook upgrade are real, dated evidence that should move the discount somewhat -
just not to peer parity.
